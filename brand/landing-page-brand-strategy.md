## Brand foundation

**Primary audience.** AI-native technical founders building developer tools or B2B software, and agent builders who need a structured creative workflow.

**Undesirable situation.** Their product is nearly ready to show but its brand is a generic prompt output, a temporary wordmark, or a slow disconnected consulting project.

**Transformation.** From a rough product truth that looks interchangeable to a clear identity system that can survive a product launch.

**Brand role.** A rigorous creative partner: part strategist, part art director, part production checklist.

**Core promise.** Turn product truth into an editable, tested brand system within an agent-led workflow.

**Reasons to believe.** Three operating modes; naming and positioning tied to the product; 50-direction creative range before selection; SVG-ready outputs; product-surface and QC gates; adapters for multiple agents.

**Personality tension.** Editorial but technical. Expressive but accountable. Open-source but considered.

**Anti-positioning.** One-click logo generator, generic AI aesthetic, decorative moodboarding, invented name mythology.

**Positioning.** For technical founders whose product needs a credible public identity, Tech Branding & Logo is the open-source agent skill that turns product truth into a usable brand system, because it connects strategy, naming, visual exploration, production assets, and QC. Unlike a logo-prompt generator, it makes every visible decision traceable to the product.

## Approved visual nouns

- **Beam** — a durable structure that carries the system.
- **Cut** — the judgement that turns abundant ideas into a selected direction.
- **Worktable** — the practical surface where creative choices become usable files.
- **Signal** — attention, clarity, and a moment of decision.
- **Route** — movement from product fact to public expression.
- **Frame** — a view that makes the important thing legible.
- **Fault line** — an interruption that reveals a meaningful distinction.
- **Stamp** — a repeatable sign of authorship and quality.
- **Field** — a flexible surface for product systems, not a decorative backdrop.

## Selected direction: Constructive Blueprint

The selected system makes strategy feel like a physical structure: a heavy ink beam, a carved stepped cut, and lime/coral signal planes. The geometry is deliberately plain enough to survive small software contexts but asymmetrical enough to avoid a generic badge. Editorial serif headlines bring considered taste; a system sans keeps the utility voice direct.

## Usage rules

- Use the full **Tech Branding & Logo** name at first mention and wherever search context matters.
- Use `TBL` as a compact visual signature, never as a replacement product name before recognition exists.
- Keep body text in ink on paper; lime and coral are signal colors, not body-text colors.
- Preserve the mark's 4-unit outer clear space and do not place another graphic inside its stepped cut.
- Use the single-ink monochrome mark for small favicons, engraving, printing, or surfaces where the colour planes are unreliable.
